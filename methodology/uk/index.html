Household data · United Kingdom

How Microcosm builds UK household data

Microcosm combines household survey records with income, wealth and spending data, then calibrates their weights against official statistics. Its local pipeline gives each household record one location and one weight, shared across national, constituency and local-authority estimates.

01 · Overview

From survey records to a weighted population

A microsimulation dataset represents households through records of their members, relationships, incomes and other characteristics. Each household has a weight: the number of households it represents in the population. PolicyEngine applies tax and benefit rules to these records and uses their weights to estimate population outcomes.

Microcosm starts with the Family Resources Survey (FRS). It preserves links between people, benefit units and households as it adds information and creates additional records. A benefit unit is the family group that many means-tested benefits assess together. The local route then assigns geographic locations and adjusts one set of weights against national and local statistics. Microcosm can also select a smaller set of records and refit their weights before packaging; those exports need their own quality checks. [2]

02 · Sources

Different sources supply different information

The FRS supplies the household structure and reported characteristics. Other sources add information that the FRS does not collect in enough detail or represent adequately on its own. The source manifest records the exact files and versions used by a build. The vintages below describe the implementation covered by this page. [3]

Additional stages use the National Travel Survey for bus use, the Effects of Taxes and Benefits dataset and National Health Service (NHS) statistics for indirect taxes and public services, and published statistics for capital gains, pension salary sacrifice and student loans. Their source coverage and assumptions are recorded separately in the manifest. For example, the recent National Travel Survey microdata cover England, so the travel stage uses declared regional proxies for devolved-country recipients. [3]

Imputation adds characteristics; calibration changes representation

Microcosm uses statistical imputation, including weighted quantile regression forests, to draw values conditional on shared characteristics such as age, income, household composition and region. The draws retain variation among otherwise similar households. They do not link an FRS household to the same identifiable household in another survey.

The income stage retains reported incomes on the original FRS records and draws income information for additional records from the Survey of Personal Incomes. Dedicated income-band records give calibration households with high incomes to work with. Wealth and consumption stages draw related variables in sequence. Calibration then changes how many households each completed record represents. [3]

03 · Geography

One location for each household record

The FRS identifies a household’s region but does not provide a representative sample for every constituency. Microcosm creates copies of completed households, keeping their people and family relationships together. Each copy receives one small census area within its original FRS region. Areas are drawn with probabilities proportional to their census household counts, using reproducible draws tied to each record’s identity. [2]

The assignment uses 2021 Census Output Areas in England and Wales, 2022 Census Output Areas in Scotland, and 2021 Census Data Zones in Northern Ireland. Published, versioned lookups determine the household’s constituency, local authority and other geographic labels from that area. The constituency geography uses the 2024 boundaries; the local-authority geography uses the April 2023 definition. [4]

Each resulting household record has one location and one weight. The same record and weight contribute to its constituency, its local authority and the national total. Constituencies and local authorities overlap, so Microcosm derives both labels from the small area rather than assuming that one sits inside the other.

These assignments create a modelled local population. They do not recover respondents’ actual addresses or establish that a record describes a particular small area accurately. The local calibration described below fits constituency and local-authority statistics; small-area assignment alone does not validate ward or neighbourhood estimates.

04 · Calibration

One set of weights, fitted across geographic levels

For each target statistic, Microcosm calculates every household’s contribution and compares the weighted total with the official value. A target might count people in an age band, sum employment income among taxpayers, or count families receiving Universal Credit. The local build fits national, constituency and local-authority targets in one optimisation. [5]

National targets cover demographic, income, tax and benefit statistics. Targets have defined populations and reference periods; HMRC income statistics, for example, describe taxpayers rather than all residents. The build records which targets it fitted, which it excluded, and how it brought source values to the target period. Available targets and coverage differ across the UK’s countries. [5]

Reconciling overlapping statistics

Published totals for the same concept can differ across geographic levels because of reference periods, definitions or census disclosure controls. Before fitting weights, Microcosm reconciles compatible, overlapping targets using an explicit precedence: country-level controls, then constituencies, then local authorities. It rescales the lower-level targets to the higher-level control and records the adjustment. This rule applies to matched concepts and declared relationships; it does not combine statistics with incompatible populations. [6]

Fitting with constraints

The optimiser adjusts weights by gradient descent to reduce a capped relative-error loss. National, constituency and local-authority targets each receive an equal share of the default local objective, preventing the larger number of local targets from overwhelming the national targets. Under the reviewed local defaults, a calibrated weight cannot exceed ten times its starting weight. These constraints can leave residual discrepancies, which the diagnostics report. [7]

For a policy comparison, PolicyEngine calculates baseline and reform outcomes for the same household records. An area’s total income change sums each assigned household’s change multiplied by its weight. With the same dataset and population definition, constituency totals sum to the national weighted total. An average divides by the relevant weighted household count; averages and percentages do not add across areas.

05 · Enhanced FRS

What changes from the earlier pipeline

The earlier Enhanced FRS pipeline used the same core survey and administrative sources. Its local calibration assigned a household different weights for many areas within its country. A household’s calculated outcomes still reflected one set of geographic inputs. Changing an area weight changed its representation in that area; it did not recalculate taxes and benefits for a new location. [8]

For example, a household outside London could contribute to a London constituency estimate while retaining the outside-London benefit cap, because the model reads the household’s region when calculating that cap. Microcosm preserves the original region when assigning each household copy a location. Its single weight follows that location across geographic levels, avoiding this mismatch between regional policy inputs and area representation. [9]

Microcosm also brings national and local targets into one calibration, expands local household and housing constraints, reconciles overlapping targets, bounds weight changes, and records local support and release checks. These changes improve geographic consistency and make the assumptions easier to inspect. They do not, on their own, establish that every local estimate is more accurate.

06 · Evidence

Read calibration fit alongside local support

Calibration diagnostics report discrepancies for individual targets and groups of targets. A headline percentage of targets within a tolerance describes fit to those targets. It does not measure the accuracy of every household characteristic or policy result.

The local pipeline also runs rotated holdouts: it refits weights while omitting a subset of local targets, then measures the omitted targets, keeping national targets in training. This checks performance on local statistics that the refit did not directly target. It is not fully independent external validation, because the omitted targets can share sources and information with the fitted targets. [10]

Support diagnostics count records with positive weights, distinct source households, and effective sample size within each area. Effective sample size summarises weight concentration: it falls when a few records carry much of an area’s weight. Copied households do not create additional independent survey observations. Record counts and effective sample size should therefore be read alongside the number of underlying source households. [10]

Release checks assess source provenance, household relationships, geography, calibration quality, local support and the exported dataset. The local release process also requires comparison evidence against the earlier pipeline. Passing software tests or completing an optimisation does not establish that a dataset has passed those release checks. Any published fit figures must identify the dataset release, target scope and diagnostic evidence they describe. [1]

Limits on interpretation

  • Finite survey support. Rare household types and narrowly defined groups can have little underlying evidence, even after copying and calibration.
  • Imputed relationships. Characteristics drawn from different sources depend on the predictors, coverage and assumptions used to connect them.
  • Untargeted outcomes. Fitting population, income or Universal Credit counts does not guarantee local accuracy for disability, savings, every benefit or every reform.
  • Model and policy assumptions. Local impacts also depend on tax and benefit rules, benefit take-up and the policy comparison. The dataset pipeline does not itself establish behavioural responses or confidence intervals.

07 · Version and references

A methodology that can be cited and checked

This draft covers the FRS 2024–25 implementation at Microcosm revision e3e3d881f0fdaf8d4796dea25ed4b72deb52cb6e. It does not report measured performance for a named dataset release. A reproducible policy analysis should also identify its dataset release, PolicyEngine-UK version, policy year and reform.

The pipeline code and methodology references are public. Several underlying surveys require licensed access; publishing the methodology does not make their respondent-level data freely redistributable. [3]
